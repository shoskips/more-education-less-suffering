I see it as a scale. On the left there's a traveler, on the right there's a tourist.

A traveler immerses themself in the culture. They learn the language and the history. They go to museums and cooperate with the locals.

A tourist uses the warm weather and unfamiliarity as a way to escape from their dull lives. They shop and eat at chain stores. They drink the same pumpkin spice latte in Milan, Shanghai and Montevideo. They put money in the pockets of the same shareholders.

While I'm opposed to both the state and capitalism, especially when the state serves capitalism (the case in 99% of countries, to differing degrees), I, along with many others, acknowledge that it's better for everyone if the money goes to independent business' instead of the state, and the state instead of corporations. We have to be more specific when we say "tourism helps the economy". A traveler is conscious of what sector of the economy they contribute to. A tourist isn't.
